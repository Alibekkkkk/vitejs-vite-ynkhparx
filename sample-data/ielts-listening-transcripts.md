# IELTS Listening Pilot: Original Recording Scripts

These short scripts are original recording prompts for the illustrative, unofficial pilot. They are not audio files. A teacher should record or generate suitable audio, upload it to a stable HTTPS location, then paste each URL into the matching Listening section in the assessment editor. The pilot stays incomplete and unpublished until audio is added.

## Section 1 — Booking a repair

**Caller:** Hello, I'd like to book a repair for my bicycle. It's a blue road bike, and the rear brake has started making a scraping sound.  
**Workshop assistant:** We can inspect it on Thursday. Could you bring it at ten in the morning?  
**Caller:** I have a class then. Is Friday afternoon possible?  
**Assistant:** Yes, we have a space at half past two. The inspection is free, but we'll call you before replacing any parts.  
**Caller:** Great. My name is Dana Ilyas, and my number is 555-0148.  
**Assistant:** Thank you. Please leave the bicycle at the side entrance on Market Street.

Suggested answer: road bike.

## Section 2 — Park visitor information

Welcome to Alder Park. The visitor centre is beside the south entrance, but today's guided walk begins at the north gate at eleven. The café near the lake is open until four. Please keep to marked paths because restoration work is taking place in the meadow. Families can borrow a simple map from the visitor centre. Drinking fountains are located beside the playground and near the north gate.

Suggested answer: The north gate.

## Section 3 — Project meeting

**Mina:** We have the survey responses now. Should we design the poster first?  
**Leo:** I think we should check the responses for missing answers before we make any charts.  
**Mina:** Good idea. Then we can compare the two age groups and decide which results are clear enough to present.  
**Leo:** I'll check the spreadsheet today. Could you ask our tutor whether the sample size is sufficient?  
**Mina:** Yes, and after that we can draft the poster together.

Suggested answer: Check the survey responses.

## Section 4 — Urban gardens

Community gardens can provide fresh produce close to where people live. When vegetables are grown and eaten locally, the distance food travels from farm to table may be shorter. Gardens also give residents a place to learn about soil, seasonal planting, and shared responsibility. They do not replace commercial farms, and their output is usually modest. Their value can include social contact and practical education as well as food. Successful projects often agree in advance how plots, tools, and harvests will be shared.

Suggested answer: farm to table.
